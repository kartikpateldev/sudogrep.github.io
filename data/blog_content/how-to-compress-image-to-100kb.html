The 100KB limit is one of the most commonly encountered file size restrictions for scanned documents and academic upload systems. Unlike the more demanding 20KB or 50KB limits, 100KB allows for higher quality output — meaning most reasonably-sized images can reach this target without visible quality degradation.

Format Comparison at 100KB

  • JPEG at 100KB: A 800px wide scanned marksheet can retain very good text legibility at JPEG quality 75–85%. Slightly yellowed paper scans hold quality well.
  • WebP at 100KB: Achieves slightly sharper edges than JPEG at the same file size. Preferred for modern portals that support WebP.
  • PNG at 100KB: For most scanned documents, PNG will not reach 100KB without resizing to a very small width. Use PNG only for simple graphics or icons.

When Quality Loss Becomes Acceptable

For scanned text documents, quality levels above 70% JPEG typically preserve all printed characters without obvious artifacts. However, photos with subtle color gradients (such as ID card photos with skin tones) require quality levels of 80% or higher to avoid noticeable banding. If you must compress a detailed photo to 100KB and the quality is still not acceptable, resize the dimensions significantly (e.g., to 600px wide) before re-compressing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I compress a scanned marksheet to 100KB without losing text legibility?
Yes. A scanned A4 marksheet at 800px wide can typically be compressed to under 100KB as a JPEG at quality 75–80% while keeping printed text readable. Avoid compressing below quality 70% for text-heavy scans.
Which image format is best for reaching 100KB?
JPEG works best for scanned documents and photographs. WebP provides similar quality at smaller sizes. Use PNG only for graphics with solid colors or transparency.
Should I resize before compressing to 100KB?
If your original image is very large (e.g., a 12MP camera photo at 4000×3000 pixels), resizing to 800–1200 pixels wide first will help you reach 100KB while keeping higher quality than aggressive direct compression.
